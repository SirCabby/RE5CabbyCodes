# RE5CabbyCodes

A cheat panel for **Resident Evil 5** on Steam (app `21690`). Pause the game and a panel appears beside the pause
menu.

## What it does

**Cheats**
- **God mode** - you take no damage, and your health is held at its maximum. You still flinch and grabs still play
  out; deaths the game scripts without a hit (a fall, a missed QTE) are left to it. The jeep you ride in chapter 2-3
  is covered too: no hit takes any of its health, and its health is held at its maximum.
- **Partner takes no damage** - the same for your partner while the computer plays them (Sheva, or Chris when you
  play Sheva). A second player's character is not covered.
- **One hit kills** - any hit that does damage kills an enemy, through the game's own death. An enemy the game keeps
  alive for now (a boss between phases) keeps its last point. The story's companions are left alone. The Ndesu (the
  giant ending chapter 2-3) goes by counters of its own: there a hit with damage takes all its strength for the round
  and lets out every Plaga still in it, and a Plaga dies of one hit - the last one's death ends the fight. Irving's
  transformed shape (the boss ending chapter 3-3) takes damage only in its core, which opens while it is down: a hit
  with damage on its body sends it down (then, or at a later hit if it is busy attacking), a hit on the open core kills
  it, and a tentacle breaks at one hit. A Reaper takes damage only in its weak spot: a hit with damage anywhere on its
  body kills it - except while it hangs from the ceiling or holds a player, when the game lets no hit hurt it.
  Uroboros Aheri (the boss ending chapter 6-2) takes damage only in its main core, which comes out once enough of
  its orange weak spots are broken: each weak spot breaks at one hit, a hit on the main core kills it, and one hit
  breaks off the attack that would otherwise kill you both.
- **Infinite ammo** - every weapon gets the game's own infinite ammo (the bonus feature's): firing uses no rounds, so
  there is nothing to reload, and a thrown grenade or egg stays in the inventory. A mounted gun (the jeep's in chapter
  2-3, a gun turret) never overheats in your hands or your partner's; the enemies' still do.
- **Steady aim** - your aim holds still: no sway while you aim or look through a scope, and no kick when you fire,
  with a gamepad or the mouse; the mouse crosshair no longer opens at a shot. A scope looks down the gun itself, which
  moved with your character's breathing: while a scope is up, that is held still too. Your shots go straight to where
  you aim, as they always do (a shotgun keeps its spread of pellets).
- **Automatic QTEs** - the quick time events play themselves. It turns on the game's own automatic mode (Action
  Assist: AUTO, whatever your setting), which answers the cutscenes' prompts and some dodges and picks the easiest
  buttons; on top of that, a grab is broken free of at once, the enemies' and bosses' dodges, holds and blows are
  taken as they come up, and a crank you take hold of winds itself all the way, as fast as the game allows (its gate
  or door fully open) - you let go (Release) when you choose, so you can hold it while your partner goes through (a
  crank that stays open by itself lets go of you, as the game always has it). Once you take hold of the boulder in the
  volcano (chapter 6-3), the pushing and the punches play out by themselves. Prompts you choose to act on (doors,
  items, melee moves, helping or commanding your partner, turning a mirror) are left to you.

**Map**
- **Open map at chapter start** - your map opens by itself as each story chapter begins (as its title comes up), and as
  Lost in Nightmares or Desperate Escape begins, just as if you had pressed the map key. The key closes it as usual, and
  the game keeps it closed from one area to the next until the next chapter begins. It takes effect from the next
  chapter start; the Mercenaries and Versus are left alone.

**Gold**
- **Infinite money** - the shop takes no gold for what you buy or upgrade; selling treasures still pays. A **gold**
  editor (the gold you set stays even if you go back to a checkpoint).

**Countdown**
- **Freeze countdown timer** - the countdown on screen stands still, so time never runs out: the Mercenaries' clock
  (its time bonuses still add to it), the escape from the oil field at the end of chapter 3-2, and the wait for the
  helicopter at the end of Desperate Escape. It runs on from there the moment you switch it off.
- While the countdown of chapter 3-2 or of Desperate Escape is running, a **time left** editor sets its seconds (up to
  99 minutes, the most the game's clock shows).

**The Mercenaries** - a section of their own, shown only during a match of The Mercenaries or The Mercenaries Reunion,
with the match's score, combo, kills and time left beside the switches. They act in a game of your own, not online.
- **Endless combo** - a combo never runs out of time: every kill counts as the next one in it however long since the
  last, so its bonus keeps climbing to its most and stays there. The game puts what a combo has earned onto your
  score only once the combo is over; with this on it goes on as it is earned instead. Switched off, the combo runs
  out as usual, 10 seconds after the last kill.
- **Combo Bonus always on** - the Combo Bonus (the 30 seconds a Combo Bonus pickup gives, in which the bonus of every
  kill from the second in a combo is at least 1,000) runs from the moment the clock starts and never runs out.
  Switched off, 30 seconds of it are left, as a pickup would leave.
- **Kill enemies as they appear** - every enemy dies the moment it is in play, wherever it is (those already about
  when you switch it on too): the game's own death, and the kill scores as one of your hand-to-hand kills (a punch, a
  kick, a finisher) - its points, the combo, the kills and the 5 seconds a hand-to-hand kill puts on the clock (a
  knife's kill earns none). The big ones (the Chainsaw Majini, the Giant Majini, the Reapers and the
  like) as well - a Chainsaw Majini stays down rather than getting up for a second round -, and the chickens: every
  stage counts its chickens among the 150 that clear it (a chicken is worth
  2,000 points). The game sends a stage's enemies area by area, only to the areas you are in; with this on every area
  sends its own wherever you stand, at the game's own pace, so a match is over in a few minutes. It waits for the
  clock to start and stops when time is up or you are down. It lasts one match: it switches itself off as the match
  ends (cleared, time up, retried or quit) - switch it on again in the pause menu of the next.
- **Freeze countdown timer** (under Countdown above) stops the Mercenaries' clock too.

**Results** (the chapter's, as the results screen shows them)
- **Freeze play time** - the chapter's clock, which its rank goes by, stands still.
- **Deaths don't count** and **Misses don't count** (a shot that misses is not counted, so the accuracy stays at
  100%).
- An editor for the chapter's time - one clock: the game counts the same time for both characters, so it is set
  for both - and editors for Chris's and Sheva's deaths, kills, shots and hits.

**Inventory** - for each character in play (Chris and Sheva, or in the extra episodes Chris or Josh and Jill):
- each item it holds, with its slot (1-9 in the 3 x 3 grid) and its count (a gun's loaded rounds, up to its
  capacity; a stack, up to what one slot holds) - type a new one and press **Apply** (or **Undo**); **Store** puts
  the item in the item box (below), and **x** throws it away (neither for the one in hand). A grenade launcher goes
  in with the rounds it has loaded. A weapon the game hands out for one scene (the Lantern, the Flamethrower, the
  L.T.D., the RPG-7 NVS and its rounds) cannot go in the box;
- tick items (or **All**) to **Store** or **Throw away** them together, and **Apply all** the counts you typed;
- **Add** - what the story gives Chris and Sheva to carry: weapons (the grenade launcher in each of its loads),
  sub-weapons (grenades, eggs, the stun rod and the rocket launcher), ammo, recovery items and vests, under tabs with
  a search box. As in the game, the Gatling gun is Chris's alone and the Longbow Sheva's. The game's own placement
  finds the item a slot, and a weapon comes loaded.
- During play, the game loads an item's model and sounds only for what an area needs (what Chris and Sheva carry as
  it loads, and what lies in it). The mod has the game load the rest itself: everything in the item box as soon as
  you pause (the game stands still, so nothing stutters), and an item here as soon as you pick it - so **Add** and
  **Take** put it in at once.

**Item box** - the game's own (54 entries), in the pause menu's panel during a chapter and in a window of its own
beside **Item Management** (between chapters):
- what it holds under tabs for each kind - weapons, sub-weapons, ammo, recovery items, armor - each tab saying how
  many it holds, with a box to narrow the list;
- the **Treasures** you hold, under a tab of their own: how many of each (up to 999) - type a new number and press
  **Apply** (or **Undo**); **x** throws a treasure away. Hover a treasure's name for what the shop pays for one.
  Treasures go in no inventory (no Take); sell them in the shop between chapters;
- each item with its count where it stacks (ammo, recovery items, grenades and eggs, up to 999 an entry) - type a
  new one and press **Apply** (or **Undo**); **x** throws it out of the box;
- **Max all** fills every count at once, under every tab: each entry of what stacks to 999, and each treasure you
  hold to 999 (a weapon keeps its rounds, and nothing new is added);
- at the pause, **Take** puts an item in the inventory picked at the top (**Take into**: Chris or Sheva): a weapon
  with its rounds and upgrades, a stack what one slot holds (the rest stays in the box). The Gatling gun goes only
  to Chris, the Longbow only to Sheva;
- tick items (**All** ticks the tab's) to **Take** or **Throw away** them together, from every tab at once, and
  **Apply all** the counts you typed;
- **Add** - weapons, sub-weapons, ammo, recovery items, vests and the game's 50 treasures, under tabs with a search
  box: tick what you want (or **Tick all listed**), say how many of each, and press **Add**. The box fills as the
  game's own does: what stacks tops up what you have, then takes empty entries; a weapon takes an entry of its own
  and comes loaded, with no upgrades; a treasure joins the treasures you hold.
- At the pause a change is kept even if you go back to a checkpoint (the treasures too), and a Store or Take stays
  made on both sides whatever comes next - **Restart**, a death and **Continue**, or **Quit** without saving: the
  item is in the box or in the inventory, once. (A Take into an inventory that was full when the game last saved
  it, with nothing in it used up since, is refused: a restart would have nowhere to put the item.)
  Beside Item Management the screen shows each change at once; changes are made while it is at its menu or you
  are browsing your inventory or the box there (not while an item is picked up or a shop list is open). Moving
  items between your inventory and the box there is the screen's own.

**Lost in Nightmares and Desperate Escape** each have an item box of their own, in the panel at the pause in that
episode. The game has no box there (each episode starts from its own loadout), so the mod keeps one for each, in a
file beside it: `RE5CabbyCodes.itembox.lost-in-nightmares.ini` and `RE5CabbyCodes.itembox.desperate-escape.ini`.
- It works as the story's box does (54 entries, the same tabs, Store, Take, Add, counts and Max all), but holds no
  treasures: the episodes have no shop. Its Add list leaves out the Longbow, and in Desperate Escape the Gatling gun
  too, since only Sheva and Chris can have them.
- Nothing in it reaches the story's box or the other episode's, and the story's box does not show up in the episodes.
- It stays as you leave it from one run of the episode to the next: store what you want to keep before you finish
  or quit, and take it again next time. **Restart** or a death and **Continue** keep each Store and Take as made.
- Every change is written to its file at once. A Store is written before the item leaves your inventory, so if the
  file cannot be written the item stays where it is.
- Partway through Lost in Nightmares the game takes everything but the knife from both inventories, as it always
  does there. What you took from the box goes with the rest, so store what you want to keep before then.
- The Mercenaries and Versus have no item box.

**Chapter Select** - a small window of its own with a **Chapter Select** button, which opens the game's Chapter
Select at its chapter list.
- **Beside Item Management** before a chapter (after a chapter's results, or after Continue, Chapter Select or New
  Game - the screen itself has no way back): it leaves the screen without starting the chapter. It stays up when you
  fold the item box window. What you changed on that screen (items moved, bought, sold or upgraded, the box and the
  treasures) is saved first, as the screen saves it when you start the chapter, so it is still there when you pick
  the next one. It waits while an item is picked up or a shop list is open.
- **At the pause** in a story chapter (beside the panel, on the pause menu's first page): it asks **Yes** or **No**
  first, then leaves the chapter as the pause menu's **Quit** does when you answer **Yes** to saving - your equipment,
  the item box and the treasures are saved as they are now, by the game's own save.
- **On the Game Over screen** (after a death, while it offers Continue or Quit): it leaves as the screen's own
  **Quit** does - the game goes back to the last checkpoint and is saved; the item box stays as it is.
- **At a chapter's results** (once their menu is up): it leaves as the results' own quit does, instead of going on
  to the next chapter - the chapter's end is saved already. After the last chapter too, instead of going back to the
  main menu.

It is there in the story only: Lost in Nightmares, Desperate Escape, Versus and the Mercenaries have no chapter list to
go back to, so the button does not show up in them.

**F7** hides or shows the panel beside the pause menu, the item box beside Item Management and Chapter Select on the
Game Over screen and a chapter's results; each is back by itself the next time its screen comes up. In play, F7
opens the panel over the game. The cheat switches are remembered between starts, and so is each window's place, size
and what you folded in it.

## Install

Works the same on Windows and on Linux (Proton), with no launch options.

1. Open the game's folder (in Steam: right-click the game > Manage > Browse local files).
2. Copy this mod's **d3d9.dll** in beside **re5dx9.exe**.

If a **d3d9.dll** is already there (another mod, such as ReShade), rename that one to **d3d9_orig.dll** first: the
mod passes the game's graphics on to it.

To uninstall: delete d3d9.dll (and rename d3d9_orig.dll back, if you had one).

It is made for the current Steam version of the game (build 11465250). On another version the mod says so in the
panel and leaves the game alone.

### If it does not work

The mod says why - in a message box as the game starts, or in the panel. Otherwise send **RE5CabbyCodes.log** and
**RE5CabbyCodes.prev.log** from the game's folder. When the game closes by itself, the log's last lines say how: the
game's own error message (a line starting `FATAL`), or where it crashed.

- **A black screen at the start, then a crash, on Linux under gamescope with `--force-windows-fullscreen`**: the
  game's fullscreen at a resolution other than gamescope's keeps resetting the display, with or without the mod.
  Give the game gamescope's resolution (its `-w` x `-h`), fullscreen or windowed: in the game's `config.ini` (in its
  Proton prefix, under `Documents/CAPCOM/RESIDENT EVIL 5/`), e.g. `Resolution=1920x1080` and, for windowed,
  `FullScreen=OFF`. The game ignores `-windowed`, `-w` and `-h` on its command line.
- **A console window reading "res = 1, err = 0x000000cb, ..."** is the game's own launcher saying it started the
  game (res = 1). It is not an error.

## Please read before using

- Much of this mod has not been played with yet: everything it uses was found in the game's own code and checked
  against it, but expect rough edges, and report them with the log.
- Changes to numbers, the inventory and the item box are made only while the game is paused - or, for the item
  box, beside Item Management, which keeps them as you leave it.
- The game saves to Steam Cloud by itself: what you change is kept by its next save.
- Owning every kind of weapon, or all four kinds of egg, earns the game's achievement for it the next time Item
  Management lists the box - whether you bought them or added them here. So does holding all 50 treasures, the next
  time it lists them.

## Config

`RE5CabbyCodes.ini` beside the DLL (written with every option documented on the first start):

```ini
ToggleKey               = 0x76  ; the key that hides/shows the panel (0x76 = F7)
GodMode                 = 0     ; each cheat's state when the game starts - the panel writes changes back here
PartnerGodMode          = 0
OneHitKills             = 0
InfiniteAmmo            = 0
SteadyAim               = 0
AutoQTE                 = 0
OpenMapAtChapterStart   = 0
InfiniteMoney           = 0
FreezePlaytime          = 0
FreezeCountdown         = 0
NoDeathCount            = 0
NoMissCount             = 0
EndlessCombo            = 0
ComboBonusAlwaysOn      = 0
KillEnemiesAsTheyAppear = 0     ; switches itself off at the end of each match
```

An ini from an earlier version is written again on the next start, with the options this one has; your values are
kept. Beside it the mod keeps `RE5CabbyCodes.log` (what it found and did) and `RE5CabbyCodes.imgui.ini` (the window's
place and size, and what is folded) - and, once you have used them, the extra episodes' item boxes:
`RE5CabbyCodes.itembox.lost-in-nightmares.ini` and `RE5CabbyCodes.itembox.desperate-escape.ini`, one line per entry
(`0x201:120` is 120 rounds of handgun ammo; a weapon's line has its loaded rounds, then its upgrade levels in hex if
it has any; what follows `;` is only the item's name). Each is written whole at every change, so edit one only while
the game is closed; deleting one empties that box. One that cannot be read is left as it is, and its box cannot be
used until the next start (the log says which line).

## Building from source

Cross-compiled on Linux with mingw-w64:

```sh
cp config.mk.example config.mk   # set GAME_DIR to your game folder
make                             # build/d3d9.dll
make install                     # installs the mod (keeps another d3d9.dll as d3d9_orig.dll)
make uninstall                   # removes it
```

`tools/` has the generators and the reverse-engineering helpers (the item table, the game's class records and their
fields, the signatures, the dump fix-up for Ghidra).
