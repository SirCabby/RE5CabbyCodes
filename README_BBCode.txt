[size=6][b]RE5CabbyCodes[/b][/size]

A [b]cheat panel[/b] for [b]Resident Evil 5[/b] on Steam. Pause the game and a panel appears beside the pause menu.

This mod is open source! Check it out at [url=https://github.com/SirCabby/RE5CabbyCodes]https://github.com/SirCabby/RE5CabbyCodes[/url]

[size=5][b]What it does[/b][/size]
[list]
[*][b]God mode[/b] - you take no damage, and your health is held at its maximum. You still flinch and grabs still play out; deaths the game scripts without a hit (a fall, a missed QTE) are left to it. The jeep you ride in chapter 2-3 is covered too: no hit takes any of its health, and its health is held at its maximum.
[*][b]Partner takes no damage[/b] - the same for your partner while the computer plays them (Sheva, or Chris when you play Sheva). A second player's character is not covered.
[*][b]One hit kills[/b] - any hit that does damage kills an enemy, through the game's own death. An enemy the game keeps alive for now (a boss between phases) keeps its last point. The story's companions are left alone. The Ndesu (the giant ending chapter 2-3) goes by counters of its own: there a hit with damage takes all its strength for the round and lets out every Plaga still in it, and a Plaga dies of one hit - the last one's death ends the fight. Irving's transformed shape (the boss ending chapter 3-3) takes damage only in its core, which opens while it is down: a hit with damage on its body sends it down (then, or at a later hit if it is busy attacking), a hit on the open core kills it, and a tentacle breaks at one hit. A Reaper takes damage only in its weak spot: a hit with damage anywhere on its body kills it - except while it hangs from the ceiling or holds a player, when the game lets no hit hurt it. Uroboros Aheri (the boss ending chapter 6-2) takes damage only in its main core, which comes out once enough of its orange weak spots are broken: each weak spot breaks at one hit, a hit on the main core kills it, and one hit breaks off the attack that would otherwise kill you both.
[*][b]Infinite ammo[/b] - every weapon gets the game's own infinite ammo (the bonus feature's): firing uses no rounds, so there is nothing to reload, and a thrown grenade or egg stays in the inventory. A mounted gun (the jeep's in chapter 2-3, a gun turret) never overheats in your hands or your partner's; the enemies' still do.
[*][b]Steady aim[/b] - your aim holds still: no sway while you aim or look through a scope, and no kick when you fire, with a gamepad or the mouse; the mouse crosshair no longer opens at a shot. A scope looks down the gun itself, which moved with your character's breathing: while a scope is up, that is held still too. Your shots go straight to where you aim, as they always do (a shotgun keeps its spread of pellets).
[*][b]Automatic QTEs[/b] - the quick time events play themselves. It turns on the game's own automatic mode (Action Assist: AUTO, whatever your setting), which answers the cutscenes' prompts and some dodges and picks the easiest buttons; on top of that, a grab is broken free of at once, the enemies' and bosses' dodges, holds and blows are taken as they come up, and a crank you take hold of winds itself all the way, as fast as the game allows (its gate or door fully open) - you let go (Release) when you choose, so you can hold it while your partner goes through (a crank that stays open by itself lets go of you, as the game always has it). Once you take hold of the boulder in the volcano (chapter 6-3), the pushing and the punches play out by themselves. Prompts you choose to act on (doors, items, melee moves, helping or commanding your partner, turning a mirror) are left to you.
[*][b]Map[/b] - [b]Open map at chapter start[/b]: your map opens by itself as each story chapter begins (as its title comes up), and as Lost in Nightmares or Desperate Escape begins, just as if you had pressed the map key. The key closes it as usual, and the game keeps it closed from one area to the next until the next chapter begins. It takes effect from the next chapter start; the Mercenaries and Versus are left alone.
[*][b]Gold[/b] - [b]Infinite money[/b] (the shop takes no gold for what you buy or upgrade; selling treasures still pays) and a gold editor (the gold you set stays even if you go back to a checkpoint).
[*][b]Countdown[/b] - [b]Freeze countdown timer[/b]: the countdown on screen stands still, so time never runs out: the Mercenaries' clock (its time bonuses still add to it), the escape from the oil field at the end of chapter 3-2, and the wait for the helicopter at the end of Desperate Escape. It runs on from there the moment you switch it off. While the countdown of chapter 3-2 or of Desperate Escape is running, a [b]time left[/b] editor sets its seconds (up to 99 minutes, the most the game's clock shows).
[*][b]The Mercenaries[/b] - a section of their own, shown only during a match of The Mercenaries or The Mercenaries Reunion, with the match's score, combo, kills and time left beside the switches. They act in a game of your own, not online. [b]Endless combo[/b]: a combo never runs out of time - every kill counts as the next one in it however long since the last, so its bonus keeps climbing to its most and stays there; the game puts what a combo has earned onto your score only once the combo is over, and with this on it goes on as it is earned instead. Switched off, the combo runs out as usual, 10 seconds after the last kill. [b]Combo Bonus always on[/b]: the Combo Bonus (the 30 seconds a Combo Bonus pickup gives, in which the bonus of every kill from the second in a combo is at least 1,000) runs from the moment the clock starts and never runs out; switched off, 30 seconds of it are left, as a pickup would leave. [b]Kill enemies as they appear[/b]: every enemy dies the moment it is in play, wherever it is (those already about when you switch it on too) - the game's own death, and the kill scores as one of your hand-to-hand kills (a punch, a kick, a finisher): its points, the combo, the kills and the 5 seconds a hand-to-hand kill puts on the clock (a knife's kill earns none). The big ones (the Chainsaw Majini, the Giant Majini, the Reapers and the like) as well - a Chainsaw Majini stays down rather than getting up for a second round -, and the chickens: every stage counts its chickens among the 150 that clear it (a chicken is worth 2,000 points). The game sends a stage's enemies area by area, only to the areas you are in; with this on every area sends its own wherever you stand, at the game's own pace, so a match is over in a few minutes. It waits for the clock to start and stops when time is up or you are down. It lasts one match: it switches itself off as the match ends (cleared, time up, retried or quit) - switch it on again in the pause menu of the next. [b]Freeze countdown timer[/b] (under Countdown) stops the Mercenaries' clock too.
[*][b]Results[/b] (the chapter's) - [b]Freeze play time[/b] (the chapter's clock, which its rank goes by, stands still), [b]Deaths don't count[/b], [b]Misses don't count[/b] (the accuracy stays at 100%), an editor for the chapter's time (one clock: the game counts the same time for both characters, so it is set for both), and editors for Chris's and Sheva's deaths, kills, shots and hits.
[*][b]Inventory[/b] - for each character in play (Chris and Sheva, or in the extra episodes Chris or Josh and Jill): each item it holds, with its slot (1-9 in the 3 x 3 grid) and its count (a gun's loaded rounds, up to its capacity; a stack, up to what one slot holds) - type a new one and press [b]Apply[/b] (or [b]Undo[/b]); [b]Store[/b] puts the item in the item box, and [b]x[/b] throws it away (neither for the one in hand). A grenade launcher goes in with the rounds it has loaded. A weapon the game hands out for one scene (the Lantern, the Flamethrower, the L.T.D., the RPG-7 NVS and its rounds) cannot go in the box. Tick items (or [b]All[/b]) to Store or Throw away them together, and [b]Apply all[/b] the counts you typed. [b]Add[/b] what the story gives Chris and Sheva to carry: weapons (the grenade launcher in each of its loads), sub-weapons (grenades, eggs, the stun rod and the rocket launcher), ammo, recovery items and vests, under tabs with a search box. As in the game, the Gatling gun is Chris's alone and the Longbow Sheva's. The game's own placement finds the item a slot, and a weapon comes loaded. During play, the game loads an item's model and sounds only for what an area needs (what Chris and Sheva carry as it loads, and what lies in it). The mod has the game load the rest itself: everything in the item box as soon as you pause (the game stands still, so nothing stutters), and an item here as soon as you pick it - so [b]Add[/b] and [b]Take[/b] put it in at once.
[*][b]Item box[/b] - the game's own (54 entries), in the pause menu's panel during a chapter and in a window of its own beside [b]Item Management[/b] (between chapters): what it holds under tabs for each kind - weapons, sub-weapons, ammo, recovery items, armor - each tab saying how many it holds, with a box to narrow the list. The [b]Treasures[/b] you hold are under a tab of their own: how many of each (up to 999) - type a new number and press [b]Apply[/b] (or [b]Undo[/b]); [b]x[/b] throws a treasure away; hover a treasure's name for what the shop pays for one. Treasures go in no inventory (no Take); sell them in the shop between chapters. Each item with its count where it stacks (ammo, recovery items, grenades and eggs, up to 999 an entry) - type a new one and press [b]Apply[/b] (or [b]Undo[/b]); [b]x[/b] throws it out of the box. [b]Max all[/b] fills every count at once, under every tab: each entry of what stacks to 999, and each treasure you hold to 999 (a weapon keeps its rounds, and nothing new is added). At the pause, [b]Take[/b] puts an item in the inventory picked at the top ([b]Take into[/b]: Chris or Sheva): a weapon with its rounds and upgrades, a stack what one slot holds (the rest stays in the box). The Gatling gun goes only to Chris, the Longbow only to Sheva. Tick items ([b]All[/b] ticks the tab's) to Take or Throw away them together, from every tab at once, and [b]Apply all[/b] the counts you typed. [b]Add[/b] weapons, sub-weapons, ammo, recovery items, vests and the game's 50 treasures, under tabs with a search box: tick what you want (or [b]Tick all listed[/b]), say how many of each, and press [b]Add[/b]. The box fills as the game's own does: what stacks tops up what you have, then takes empty entries; a weapon takes an entry of its own and comes loaded, with no upgrades; a treasure joins the treasures you hold. At the pause a change is kept even if you go back to a checkpoint (the treasures too), and a Store or Take stays made on both sides whatever comes next - [b]Restart[/b], a death and [b]Continue[/b], or [b]Quit[/b] without saving: the item is in the box or in the inventory, once. (A Take into an inventory that was full when the game last saved it, with nothing in it used up since, is refused: a restart would have nowhere to put the item.) Beside Item Management the screen shows each change at once; changes are made while it is at its menu or you are browsing your inventory or the box there (not while an item is picked up or a shop list is open). Moving items between your inventory and the box there is the screen's own.
[*][b]Lost in Nightmares and Desperate Escape[/b] each have an item box of their own, in the panel at the pause in that episode. The game has no box there (each episode starts from its own loadout), so the mod keeps one for each, in a file beside it: [b]RE5CabbyCodes.itembox.lost-in-nightmares.ini[/b] and [b]RE5CabbyCodes.itembox.desperate-escape.ini[/b]. It works as the story's box does (54 entries, the same tabs, Store, Take, Add, counts and Max all), but holds no treasures: the episodes have no shop. Its Add list leaves out the Longbow, and in Desperate Escape the Gatling gun too, since only Sheva and Chris can have them. Nothing in it reaches the story's box or the other episode's, and the story's box does not show up in the episodes. It stays as you leave it from one run of the episode to the next: store what you want to keep before you finish or quit, and take it again next time. [b]Restart[/b] or a death and [b]Continue[/b] keep each Store and Take as made. Every change is written to its file at once. A Store is written before the item leaves your inventory, so if the file cannot be written the item stays where it is. Partway through Lost in Nightmares the game takes everything but the knife from both inventories, as it always does there. What you took from the box goes with the rest, so store what you want to keep before then. The Mercenaries and Versus have no item box.
[*][b]Chapter Select[/b] - a small window of its own with a [b]Chapter Select[/b] button, which opens the game's Chapter Select at its chapter list. [b]Beside Item Management[/b] before a chapter (after a chapter's results, or after Continue, Chapter Select or New Game - the screen itself has no way back): it leaves the screen without starting the chapter. It stays up when you fold the item box window. What you changed on that screen (items moved, bought, sold or upgraded, the box and the treasures) is saved first, as the screen saves it when you start the chapter, so it is still there when you pick the next one. It waits while an item is picked up or a shop list is open. [b]At the pause[/b] in a story chapter (beside the panel, on the pause menu's first page): it asks [b]Yes[/b] or [b]No[/b] first, then leaves the chapter as the pause menu's [b]Quit[/b] does when you answer [b]Yes[/b] to saving - your equipment, the item box and the treasures are saved as they are now, by the game's own save. [b]On the Game Over screen[/b] (after a death, while it offers Continue or Quit): it leaves as the screen's own [b]Quit[/b] does - the game goes back to the last checkpoint and is saved; the item box stays as it is. [b]At a chapter's results[/b] (once their menu is up): it leaves as the results' own quit does, instead of going on to the next chapter - the chapter's end is saved already. After the last chapter too, instead of going back to the main menu. It is there in the story only: Lost in Nightmares, Desperate Escape, Versus and the Mercenaries have no chapter list to go back to, so the button does not show up in them.
[*][b]F7[/b] hides or shows the panel beside the pause menu, the item box beside Item Management and Chapter Select on the Game Over screen and a chapter's results; each is back by itself the next time its screen comes up. In play, F7 opens the panel over the game. The cheat switches are remembered between starts, and so is each window's place, size and what you folded in it.
[/list]

[size=5][b]Install[/b][/size]

Works the same on Windows and on Linux (Proton), with no launch options. Open the game's folder (in Steam: right-click the game > Manage > Browse local files) and copy this mod's [b]d3d9.dll[/b] in beside [b]re5dx9.exe[/b].

If a [b]d3d9.dll[/b] is already there (another mod, such as ReShade), rename that one to [b]d3d9_orig.dll[/b] first: the mod passes the game's graphics on to it.

To uninstall: delete d3d9.dll (and rename d3d9_orig.dll back, if you had one).

It is made for the current Steam version of the game (build 11465250). On another version the mod says so in the panel and leaves the game alone.

[size=5][b]If it does not work[/b][/size]

The mod says why - in a message box as the game starts, or in the panel. Otherwise send [b]RE5CabbyCodes.log[/b] and [b]RE5CabbyCodes.prev.log[/b] from the game's folder. When the game closes by itself, the log's last lines say how: the game's own error message (a line starting FATAL), or where it crashed.
[list]
[*][b]A black screen at the start, then a crash, on Linux under gamescope with --force-windows-fullscreen[/b]: the game's fullscreen at a resolution other than gamescope's keeps resetting the display, with or without the mod. Give the game gamescope's resolution (its -w x -h), fullscreen or windowed: in the game's config.ini (in its Proton prefix, under Documents/CAPCOM/RESIDENT EVIL 5/), e.g. Resolution=1920x1080 and, for windowed, FullScreen=OFF. The game ignores -windowed, -w and -h on its command line.
[*][b]A console window reading "res = 1, err = 0x000000cb, ..."[/b] is the game's own launcher saying it started the game (res = 1). It is not an error.
[/list]

[size=5][b]Please read before using[/b][/size]
[list]
[*]Much of this mod has not been played with yet: everything it uses was found in the game's own code and checked against it, but expect rough edges, and report them with the log.
[*]Changes to numbers, the inventory and the item box are made only while the game is paused - or, for the item box, beside Item Management, which keeps them as you leave it.
[*]The game saves to Steam Cloud by itself: what you change is kept by its next save.
[*]Owning every kind of weapon, or all four kinds of egg, earns the game's achievement for it the next time Item Management lists the box - whether you bought them or added them here. So does holding all 50 treasures, the next time it lists them.
[/list]

[size=5][b]Config[/b][/size]

[code]
ToggleKey               = 0x76  ; the key that hides/shows the panel (0x76 = F7)
GodMode                 = 0     ; each cheat's state when the game starts - the panel writes changes back here
PartnerGodMode          = 0
OneHitKills             = 0
InfiniteAmmo            = 0
SteadyAim               = 0
AutoQTE                 = 0
OpenMapAtChapterStart   = 0
InfiniteMoney           = 0
FreezePlaytime          = 0
FreezeCountdown         = 0
NoDeathCount            = 0
NoMissCount             = 0
EndlessCombo            = 0
ComboBonusAlwaysOn      = 0
KillEnemiesAsTheyAppear = 0     ; switches itself off at the end of each match
[/code]

An ini from an earlier version is written again on the next start, with the options this one has; your values are kept. Beside it the mod keeps [b]RE5CabbyCodes.log[/b] (what it found and did) and [b]RE5CabbyCodes.imgui.ini[/b] (the window's place and size, and what is folded) - and, once you have used them, the extra episodes' item boxes: [b]RE5CabbyCodes.itembox.lost-in-nightmares.ini[/b] and [b]RE5CabbyCodes.itembox.desperate-escape.ini[/b], one line per entry ([b]0x201:120[/b] is 120 rounds of handgun ammo; a weapon's line has its loaded rounds, then its upgrade levels in hex if it has any; what follows ; is only the item's name). Each is written whole at every change, so edit one only while the game is closed; deleting one empties that box. One that cannot be read is left as it is, and its box cannot be used until the next start (the log says which line).
